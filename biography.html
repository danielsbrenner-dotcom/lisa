Biography

Brenner is a Professor of Theatre Arts and the past Chair of the Theatre & Dance Department of Drew University. An award-winning educator, Brenner is a recipient of the President’s Award for Distinguished Teaching and the Faculty Leadership Civic Engagement Award, both from Drew University. From 2017-2019 she served as lead editor of Theatre Topics, the official journal of the Association for Theatre in Higher Education, published by Johns Hopkins University Press.

Brenner is best known for her documentary-based play Katrina: the K Word (co-written with Suzanne Trauth) which has been presented on campuses in over fourteen states. It is included in Katrina on Stage: Five Plays (Northwestern University Press), which Brenner co-edited.

In addition to her artistic work, Brenner is the editor of the book Playing Harry Potter: Essays and Interviews on Fandom and Performance. With Evelyn Diaz Cruz, she is co-editor of Routledge’s Applied Theatre in Context book series, which includes Applied Theatre with Youth: Education, Engagement, Activism (co-edited with Chris Ceraso and Diaz Cruz) and Applied Theatre and Gender Justice: Imagination, Play, Movement (co-edited with Diaz Cruz). Her published articles include “Playing Jewish at the National Asian American Theatre Company” and “Beyond Words: Producing Palestinian–Israeli Dialogue at the Galilee Multicultural Theatre,” both in Theatre Topics, as well as “Storming the Nation: Post-Katrina New Orleans, Documentary Theatre, and Civic Responsibility,” which ran in Transformations.

Brenner graduated Summa Cum Laude from Barnard College, where she started Student Theater at Barnard, a student-run initiative that created new works. Upon graduation she created the Philadelphia Women’s Ensemble Theater Company, a professional company that produced mainstage productions and ran a community outreach program for local schools for over a decade. She returned to New York City to study with Anne Bogart, Holly Hughes, and the late Augusto Boal and to earn a doctorate in Dramatic Literature from Columbia University. Before teaching at Drew, she taught courses at Columbia, Montclair State University, and Bloomfield College.
